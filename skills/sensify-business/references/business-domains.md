# Business Domain Prompts and Failure Modes

Use this reference to broaden coverage after high-leverage frontier questions are handled. Do not convert it into a checklist interview.

## Purpose / founder fit

Probe:

- What outcome is the founder optimizing for: income, enterprise value, stability, impact, autonomy, growth, exit, or something else?
- Which constraints are genuinely non-negotiable?
- Does the proposed model require capital, risk, scale, or management complexity inconsistent with founder goals?

Failure modes:

- business model succeeds financially but creates an unwanted operating life;
- growth assumptions conflict with desired ownership/control;
- founder capabilities are treated as infinitely scalable.

## Customer / buyer

Probe:

- What objective characteristics define a qualified customer?
- Who feels the pain versus who pays?
- Who can block a purchase?
- What event causes the buyer to reconsider the status quo?

Failure modes:

- user likes product but buyer has no budget;
- segment is too broad to create a repeatable sales motion;
- business targets accounts with pain but no authority/urgency.

## Problem

Probe:

- How often does the problem occur?
- What does it cost today?
- What happens if nothing changes?
- How do customers currently cope?
- Is the problem painful enough to overcome switching cost?

Failure modes:

- problem is real but too infrequent to support demand;
- workaround is "good enough";
- problem is severe only for a small subset not identified by targeting.

## Competition / alternatives

Probe:

- What would the customer do if this company did not exist?
- Why is an internal employee, existing vendor, spreadsheet, bundled software, or inaction not sufficient?
- What switching risk does the customer perceive?

Failure modes:

- founder defines competition too narrowly;
- differentiation exists but is not valuable to the buyer;
- customer risk of switching exceeds expected benefit.

## Value proposition

Probe:

- What measurable or felt outcome changes?
- Why does the buyer care now?
- Why can this company deliver the outcome credibly?
- Which promise creates liability or unrealistic expectations?

Failure modes:

- feature-led pitch without business outcome;
- differentiation is visible only to the founder;
- promise depends on factors outside the company's control.

## Offering

Probe:

- What is explicitly included/excluded?
- Which exceptions create hidden labor?
- What customer cooperation is required?
- Where does bespoke work creep in?

Failure modes:

- service scope expands faster than price;
- implementation effort is ignored;
- exceptions destroy repeatability.

## Pricing / revenue

Probe:

- What is the pricing unit and why does it align with customer value and delivery cost?
- What evidence supports willingness to pay?
- Does the contract create adverse incentives?
- How do price, discounts, commissions, and service cost interact?

Failure modes:

- price copied from competitors without economics/value logic;
- revenue grows while contribution margin degrades;
- lifetime commissions or discounts consume future margin.

## Sales / acquisition

Probe:

- Where do qualified leads come from repeatedly?
- What proof is needed to close?
- What is the likely sales cycle?
- How are salespeople rewarded for quality, retention, and margin?

Failure modes:

- acquisition channel works only through founder network;
- sales compensation rewards bad-fit deals;
- CAC or sales effort is omitted because early sales are relationship-driven.

## Operations

Probe:

- What is the critical path for delivery?
- What happens when data is late, wrong, or missing?
- Where are manual judgment and exception handling required?
- What capacity unit constrains growth?
- What must never fail?

Failure modes:

- happy-path process mistaken for operating model;
- single person/vendor can stop service;
- controls arrive after scale instead of before critical risk.

## Economics

Probe:

- What cost changes with each customer/unit?
- What costs are hidden in onboarding, support, rework, refunds, commissions, or compliance?
- How does retention affect lifetime economics?
- When does cash leave versus arrive?

Failure modes:

- revenue forecast masks negative unit economics;
- gross margin excludes real recurring labor;
- growth increases cash needs faster than cash generation.

## Organization

Probe:

- Which capability has no owner?
- Which role is a key-person dependency?
- Which decisions must be centralized versus delegated?

Failure modes:

- founder remains permanent bottleneck;
- accountability is diffuse;
- critical compliance/quality work is treated as incidental.

## Technology / data

Probe:

- Which technology assumptions are business-critical?
- What happens during outages or integration failure?
- Who can access sensitive data?
- What manual fallback exists?

Failure modes:

- vendor/platform capability assumed rather than verified;
- automation hides exception-handling labor;
- data security is treated as an IT detail instead of business risk.

## Regulation / compliance

Probe:

- Which authority governs the activity?
- Which obligations change by jurisdiction/customer type?
- What records/audits/filings are required?
- Who bears liability when the service makes an error?

Failure modes:

- mainland/other-jurisdiction assumptions applied locally;
- compliance cost absent from economics;
- contract cannot transfer statutory responsibility as assumed.

## Risk / validation

Probe:

- Which single assumption, if false, most damages the model?
- What can be tested cheaply before committing heavily?
- What evidence would actually cause us to change course?

Failure modes:

- validation seeks confirmation rather than disconfirmation;
- easy-to-test low-impact hypotheses receive attention while critical beliefs remain untested;
- research evidence is mistaken for customer commitment.
