# Business Validation Framework

Validation converts critical business hypotheses into evidence. Sensify identifies validation needs and designs the evidence requirement; execution can be delegated to downstream work.

## Prioritize what could kill the model

Rank validation needs primarily by:

- business impact if false;
- current uncertainty;
- amount of downstream commitment dependent on the claim;
- cost of discovering the truth later versus now.

Do not spend early validation effort perfecting low-impact details.

## Evidence ladder

Different questions require different evidence. Roughly, evidence becomes stronger as it moves from stated preference toward costly revealed behavior.

Possible forms include:

1. secondary/public research;
2. expert/operator evidence;
3. qualitative customer interviews;
4. observed current behavior/workarounds;
5. pricing/concept tests;
6. pilot participation;
7. letters of intent or procurement action;
8. paid trial/paid pilot;
9. repeat usage/renewal;
10. durable retention and unit economics.

This is not a rigid universal hierarchy. Use evidence appropriate to the hypothesis.

## Hypothesis specification

A useful validation item should state:

```text
Hypothesis:
<claim>

Why it matters:
<decision/model component depending on it>

Current evidence:
<what we actually know>

Evidence needed:
<what would meaningfully increase confidence>

Disconfirming evidence:
<what would cause us to change course>

Proposed method:
<research/interview/experiment/pilot/etc.>
```

## Customer interviews

When interviews are appropriate, design them to learn about actual context and behavior rather than solicit approval for the idea.

Prefer questions about:

- recent concrete events;
- current process/workaround;
- cost/time/risk experienced;
- who was involved;
- what triggered action;
- what alternatives were considered;
- what prevented action;
- actual budgets/procurement where appropriate.

Avoid treating compliments, hypothetical enthusiasm, or "I would use that" as strong demand evidence.

## Validation vs research

External research can establish facts about the environment and can strengthen plausibility. It often cannot validate context-specific behavioral claims such as:

- this segment will switch;
- this buyer will pay this price;
- this onboarding process is acceptable;
- this sales channel will convert economically.

Keep those as hypotheses until appropriate target-context evidence exists.

## Stop condition for Sensify

Sensify does not need to execute every validation item before completion.

It does need to make clear:

- which critical claims remain unvalidated;
- what evidence would be sufficient for the next decision;
- which commitments should wait for that evidence;
- which risks the user consciously accepts without validation.
