# Business Red-Team Framework

Use red-teaming to locate load-bearing weaknesses in the business model. The objective is not pessimism. It is to expose beliefs whose failure would materially change the decision.

## Red-team lenses

### Desirability

Ask:

- Is the problem painful/frequent/urgent enough to motivate action?
- Does the proposed customer actually perceive the problem the way the founder does?
- What evidence demonstrates demand rather than interest?
- Why would a buyer switch now?

### Willingness to pay

Ask:

- Who controls the budget?
- What economic or operational value justifies the price?
- What evidence supports the proposed pricing range?
- Can the buyer capture the benefit, or does another stakeholder receive it?

### Status quo strength

Ask:

- Why is doing nothing acceptable today?
- What workaround must be displaced?
- What switching costs, retraining, integrations, contracts, trust, or risk protect the incumbent/status quo?

### Feasibility

Ask:

- Can the service/product actually deliver the promised outcome under normal and exceptional conditions?
- Which capabilities are assumed but not present?
- Which external dependencies can fail?

### Viability

Ask:

- Does each incremental customer/unit create enough contribution to support the business?
- Which hidden labor/cost grows with scale?
- Does growth increase cash strain?
- Are commissions, partner shares, refunds, implementation, and support included?

### Incentives

Ask:

- What behavior does each compensation/KPI/contract actually reward?
- Can someone improve their metric while harming customer fit, quality, margin, compliance, or retention?

### Scalability

Ask:

- Which activity grows roughly one-for-one with revenue/customers?
- Where is founder judgment embedded in the process?
- What exception rate would break the model?

### Defensibility / substitution

Ask:

- If the model works, how easily can an incumbent, employee, software vendor, or new entrant reproduce the value?
- Does defensibility matter for the founder's actual objective/time horizon?

### Regulation / liability

Ask:

- Which promise creates legal, financial, fiduciary, privacy, or compliance exposure?
- What responsibility cannot be contractually delegated?
- How large can a single failure become?

### Concentration

Ask:

- Does one customer, salesperson, founder, vendor, platform, data source, or jurisdiction create disproportionate dependency?

### Timing

Ask:

- Why now?
- What changes if the customer waits six months?
- Which timing assumption can invalidate the launch or economics?

## Premortem exercise

At major milestones, generate a short premortem:

> It is 24 months later and the business failed despite competent effort. What happened?

Create plausible failure paths across:

- no/weak demand;
- poor retention;
- price resistance;
- acquisition economics;
- delivery cost;
- quality failure;
- compliance/liability;
- cash shortage;
- key-person dependency;
- vendor/platform failure;
- founder/team mismatch;
- competition/status quo;
- execution sequencing.

For each material failure path:

1. identify the underlying assumption/decision;
2. record early warning indicators if knowable;
3. determine whether research or validation can reduce uncertainty now;
4. create/update a risk or frontier question.

## Reconsideration discipline

When a red-team finding materially weakens an existing decision, invoke the Sensify decision-reopening protocol.

Do not merely add a risk note while leaving the contradicted decision untouched.
