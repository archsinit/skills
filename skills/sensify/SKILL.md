# Sensify

Start when the user explicitly asks for Sensify or for this kind of understanding-building interview. Complexity alone is not a trigger. For business work, also use `sensify-business` when available.

The aim is a model the user can recognize and use to make decisions. It must explain both the outcome they want and how they currently expect to reach it. Keep facts, private information, decisions, assumptions, evidence, contradictions, and unknowns distinct. The model can be ready while some hypotheses remain untested; it cannot be ready while consequential uncertainty is hidden.

## Start with what is known

Read the current initiative's model and relevant source material before asking the user to repeat anything. Establish the outcome, reason it matters, proposed approach, scope, constraints, success criteria, time horizon, existing decisions, and terms whose meaning could change the work. Pursue only gaps that matter.

Use [the knowledge model](references/knowledge-model.md) to type material claims and track their sources and dependencies. Keep the version the user reads coherent. Give structured records and IDs only to claims that need evidence, history, or links to other claims.

Research facts you can reasonably find in files, connected sources, public material, or calculations. Ask the user for private facts, judgments, preferences, and decisions. Outside research may inform a hypothesis about the user's context without proving it there. Follow [the research protocol](references/research-protocol.md).

## Work through the live questions

Map what remains unresolved and what depends on it. Ask questions whose prerequisites are settled enough to answer. Give priority to what could cause the most harm if wrong, unlock the most later work, or prove costly to reverse. [The questioning engine](references/questioning-engine.md) describes this frontier.

Favor fewer, fuller rounds of independent questions that the user can answer by dictation. A round may exceed seven questions if the questions belong together. Ask fewer when one answer could change the rest. For a material choice, give your current read and recommendation, with the reason, main tradeoff, and uncertainty. The user makes the decision.

After each meaningful answer or research result, update the model and the question frontier. Record material sources and dates, decision rationale, dependencies, contradictions, and conditions that would justify reopening a decision. Preserve a consequential old state as superseded instead of silently replacing it. Follow [the persistence protocol](references/persistence-protocol.md); do not wait until the interview ends.

## Challenge the model

Challenge weak reasoning, including the stated goal when that could materially change the work. Look for unsupported certainty, hidden premises, conflicting claims, vague terms, missing stakeholders, bad incentives, and solutions chosen before the problem is understood. Periodically audit assumptions and contradictions, then ask which current belief would most likely explain failure. Use [the challenge protocol](references/challenge-protocol.md).

A decision can be reopened when new evidence, a changed constraint, a contradiction, or a downstream consequence weakens its rationale. Explain what changed and what keeping the decision would mean; recommend a course, then let the user choose. Do not relitigate it without a new material basis.

## Finish or hand off

Use [the completion criteria](references/completion-criteria.md) to judge readiness for the next work. Present a compact account of the intended outcome and mechanism, settled decisions, critical assumptions, risks, open questions, and validation still needed. Ask the user whether it accurately represents their intent before declaring Sensify complete.

Do not begin substantial downstream implementation while the interview is active. The user may end or suspend it at any time. Record consequential gaps, make clear that the model has not passed the completion test, and follow their direction.
